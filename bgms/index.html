The knowledge exists. The translation didn't — until now.

Most green-building knowledge is locked inside standards, rating systems, and technical papers written for specialists. BGMS keeps the rigour and removes the barrier: every recommendation is weighed against three plain filters before it earns a place on the page.

Filter 01

Economy

Can a household actually afford it? Costs are given in real ranges, with payback where it matters — not aspirational figures.

Filter 02

Ease

Can it be done without specialist skills or equipment? If a measure needs a professional, we say so plainly.

Filter 03

Climate fit

Does it actually work where you live? Every measure is flagged to the climate zones where it delivers — and where it doesn't.

Six climate zones

Find the climate you build in.

Recommendations are organised by zone, because what cools a home in Jaipur can trap damp in Halifax. Each zone carries its own dominant comfort and indoor-air challenge.

Hot-Humid
Chennai · Singapore · Miami · Lagos

Challenge: rejecting heat while managing moisture, mould, and ventilation.

Hot-Dry
Jaipur · Phoenix · Cairo · Alice Springs

Challenge: daytime heat and dust; thermal mass and shading do the heavy lifting.

Temperate
London · Melbourne · Vancouver · Tokyo

Challenge: mixed seasons; balancing heating, cooling, and fresh air year-round.

Cold / Subarctic
Halifax · Toronto · Oslo · Harbin

Challenge: retaining heat and avoiding condensation in a tight, well-insulated shell.

High Altitude
Leh · La Paz · Bogotá · Lhasa

Challenge: intense sun, cold nights, thin air; thermal mass and solar gain together.

Mixed / Composite
Delhi · Beijing · Madrid · Kabul

Challenge: hot summers and cold winters; strategies must flex across both.

Wisdom from the past

Centuries of climate intelligence, explained as physics.

BGMS treats vernacular building as substantive knowledge — naming the tradition, explaining how it works, and drawing the equivalence to modern practice.

Rajasthani jaali

The perforated stone screen accelerates air as it squeezes through each opening — the Venturi effect — cooling the room behind it. Hot-dry, refined over four centuries.

Himalayan thermal mass

Thick earth and stone walls — the logic of Kath-Kuni and Ladakhi homes — store the day's warmth and release it through cold high-altitude nights.

Courtyards & wind-catchers

North African courtyards and Persian badgirs move air without power, pulling cool night air down and venting heat by day.

How a page reads

One figure. One plain sentence. One action.

Key Number
37%

of global energy-related CO₂ emissions come from constructing and running buildings — more than transport.

Source: UN Global Status Report for Buildings & Construction (2023)

Quick Win

Buy a CO₂ meter (~$30–80). Leave it in your bedroom for a week. If readings regularly pass 1,000 ppm with the door shut, that room needs ventilation.

Why you can trust it

Rigour you can check, in language you can use.

  • Tiered sourcing. Every figure traces to a Tier 1 (peer-reviewed, official standards and bodies) or Tier 2 (reputable industry) source — never an unsourced number.
  • A public research log. The evidence behind the guide is documented and open, so claims can be traced and challenged.
  • Certification-agnostic. BGMS leads with cost and climate, not a single rating system — the fundamentals apply under LEED, WELL, EDGE, or none.
  • Open access, freely shared. Released under Creative Commons so educators and communities can use and distribute it.
